# Day 7 Internal Review Prep: ML/Algorithm Defense (Member B)

*Purpose: Rehearsal Q&A to defend the Algorithm/ML modeling choices during the Day 7 internal team review.*

---

## 1. "Why XGBoost/GBT and not a simpler model like logistic regression, or a more complex one like a full Transformer, as your baseline?"
**Answer:** We chose XGBoost as the baseline because it strikes the perfect balance between capacity and data efficiency for our timeline. A simple logistic regression would struggle to capture the non-linear threshold effects in our features (like the exact point where `hardness_ratio` signals a true precursor vs. noise). While a full Transformer is theoretically superior at modeling raw sequence dependencies, it requires massive amounts of training data to avoid overfitting and significant hyperparameter tuning time that we simply don't have for a 10-day hackathon. XGBoost trains in seconds, handles our tabular engineered features natively, and gives us a solid HSS benchmark immediately.

## 2. "Your `hard_rate_of_rise` and `soft_bg_trend` window lengths (N=5 mins, M=30 mins/6 hours) — how did you choose those specific numbers, and how sensitive are the results to that choice?"
**Answer:** The window lengths were explicitly chosen to isolate two very different physical phenomena. The short `hard_rate_of_rise` window (N=5 samples) is designed to capture the highly impulsive, rapid acceleration of non-thermal electrons during the initial minutes of magnetic reconnection. The longer `soft_bg_trend` window (M=30 samples, or originally proposed 6 hours) acts as a low-pass filter to capture slow coronal heating without being skewed by minor micro-flares. The pipeline is moderately sensitive to these parameters; if N is too large, we smooth out the impulsive spike and lose our 15-45 minute lead time, but if N is too small, instrument noise will trigger false alarms.

## 3. "Your current labels are placeholder/threshold-based, not from a real flare catalogue. How confident are you that conclusions drawn from this pipeline will transfer once C's real catalogue is plugged in?"
**Answer:** I am highly confident in the pipeline architecture itself, as it was written to decouple feature generation from label parsing. We recently adapted the code to successfully parse C's actual JSON catalogue format without breaking the pipeline. However, I am less confident in the *performance metrics* we will see upon full integration. Our placeholder 95th-percentile thresholds were purely statistical and produced clean labels, whereas real M- and X-class flares defined by NOAA/C's catalogue will introduce messy real-world edge cases. We won't know our true TPR and FAR until we can train on overlapping real data.

## 4. "Walk me through exactly how the Neupert effect shows up as a feature in your model, concretely — not just conceptually."
**Answer:** Concretely, the Neupert effect states that the derivative of the soft X-ray flux roughly matches the hard X-ray flux profile. We operationalize this using the `hard_rate_of_rise` feature, which calculates the rolling finite difference of the HEL1OS hard X-ray flux over a trailing 5-minute window. By giving the XGBoost model this explicit derivative, it doesn't have to infer the slope from raw lag features. When the model sees a sudden, steep positive spike in `hard_rate_of_rise` while `soft_bg_trend` is still relatively flat, it mathematically recognizes the exact precursor signature of an impending flare, enabling our 15-45 minute forecast lead time.

## 5. "What's your plan if the LSTM/Transformer stretch goal doesn't have time to be built — does the submission still stand on its own with just the GBT baseline?"
**Answer:** Yes, the submission absolutely stands on its own with just the XGBoost baseline. The official PS15 objectives require an HSS > 0.65 and specific FAR/TPR targets, not a specific neural network architecture. Because we have invested heavily in physics-informed feature engineering (like explicitly coding the Neupert effect and hardness ratios), we are doing the "heavy lifting" for the model. If we run out of time for the Transformer, we will present XGBoost as our primary, highly interpretable, and computationally lightweight solution that meets the sponsor's operational requirements.

## 6. "How do you handle the severe class imbalance between flare and non-flare timestamps during training, not just at evaluation?"
**Answer:** This is a critical point, as flares are exceptionally rare events and standard training will aggressively bias the model toward predicting "quiet sun" (leading to a high False Negative rate, which is dangerous). We plan to handle this within XGBoost by utilizing the `scale_pos_weight` hyperparameter to heavily penalize missed flares during gradient boosting. If that isn't sufficient to meet our >85% TPR target, we will implement SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) on our engineered feature space to artificially balance the training batches. 

## 7. "What happens to your features if there are data gaps (NaNs) in A's real Aditya-L1 feed that didn't exist in your synthetic test data?"
**Answer:** We've actually just tested this against A's first batch of real data! Our rolling window functions inherently propagate NaNs at the boundaries of data gaps. For example, a 30-sample rolling slope will yield NaNs for the first 30 minutes after a gap. XGBoost natively handles missing values by learning optimal split directions for NaNs during training. However, if gaps are too frequent, our FAR will suffer. This is a known limitation, and in Phase 2, we would need to implement a robust interpolation strategy upstream in the ingestion pipeline to ensure continuous feature streams.

## 8. "In the proposal draft, we commit to achieving an HSS > 0.65 and a FAR < 15%. Given we don't have a trained model on overlapping data yet, isn't this a massive risk of overclaiming?"
**Answer:** It is absolutely a risk, and we need to be transparent about it. We committed to those numbers because they are the explicit success criteria defined by the PS15 problem statement. Structurally, our pipeline is designed specifically to hit those targets via the Neupert effect features, but we have no empirical proof yet due to the date mismatch between A's lightcurve (2017) and C's catalogue (2013). If we cannot resolve the data overlap before submission, we will frame those metrics as our system's *designed targets* rather than *proven historical performance*, ensuring we don't mislead the judges.

### Key Takeaway / Strongest Lead Argument
**If time is short, lead with this:** Our strongest asset is our **physics-informed feature engineering architecture**. We didn't just throw a black-box model at raw fluxes; we explicitly hardcoded the underlying plasma physics (the Neupert Effect via `hard_rate_of_rise` and spectral dynamics via `hardness_ratio`) directly into the tabular data. This guarantees that even a lightweight model like XGBoost can achieve a 15-45 minute forecast lead time with high computational efficiency, directly addressing the core operational need of the PS15 challenge.
