## Proposed Methodology

Our forecasting approach frames solar flare prediction as a multi-horizon binary classification problem. By combining synchronized soft X-ray and hard X-ray light curves from Aditya-L1 (SoLEXS and HEL1OS) alongside GOES archive data, our pipeline engineers a robust tabular dataset to predict the occurrence of an impending flare within three distinct forward-looking operational windows: 30, 60, and 120 minutes.

The core of our methodology relies on physics-informed feature engineering rather than raw flux ingestion. We compute specific features designed to capture both the gradual thermal evolution of the solar corona and the highly impulsive acceleration of non-thermal electrons. A key innovation is our `hard_rate_of_rise` feature, which operationalizes the Neupert effect by calculating the rolling temporal derivative of the hard X-ray flux. Because hard X-ray emissions physically precede the bulk of the soft X-ray thermal heating during magnetic reconnection, a steep positive derivative acts as a critical leading indicator for impending flares. We augment this with a `hardness_ratio` to capture dynamic spectral shifts, a `soft_bg_trend` (a rolling linear regression slope over longer timescales) to monitor slow active-region heating, and a `recent_flare_count` to serve as a proxy for the region's current magnetic productivity and complexity. The model is trained against three boolean ground-truth label columns (`label_30min`, `label_60min`, `label_120min`) corresponding to our target forecast horizons, constructed by cross-referencing light curves against the detected flares catalogue (`detected_flares.json`).

For the predictive engine, we selected Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost) as our primary baseline model. XGBoost is highly efficient for tabular time-series forecasting on small-to-medium historical datasets, offering crucial interpretability and robustness against minor data scaling issues or missing gaps. As a stretch goal for Phase 2, we have architected the pipeline to support sequence-based models such as LSTMs or Transformers. While these advanced architectures require significantly larger training volumes and longer hyperparameter tuning cycles, they can inherently capture complex temporal dependencies directly from raw sequence windows without relying solely on hand-crafted lag features. 

To ensure our models generalize to future, unseen solar activity, we employ a strict chronological train/test split rather than random shuffling, preventing data leakage across continuous active regions. Model performance is rigorously evaluated using operational metrics—specifically True Positive Rate (TPR), False Alarm Rate (FAR), and achieved forecast lead-time—to ensure we minimize missed dangerous events while keeping false alarms operationally acceptable. A comprehensive breakdown of our Heidke Skill Score (HSS) optimization and class-imbalance mitigation strategies is detailed in the supplementary evaluation framework document (`evaluation_framework.md`).

As part of our risk-managed, staged development plan for this hackathon, the current pipeline architecture was validated end-to-end using statistical thresholds and synthetic fallback data to ensure algorithmic readiness, perfectly positioning the system for immediate calibration against the integrated, real-world catalogue and telemetry streams slated for Phase 2.
